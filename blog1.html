Why We Still Scoop from Pozzetti

Walk into any gelateria in Northern Italy and you'll find the same thing behind the counter: rounded steel pozzetti, lids on, gelato tucked away from the light and air. No towering domes, no piped swirls. Just simple, covered tubs — and there's a reason we've kept it that way here in Rosalie Village.

Pozzetti aren't just a stylistic choice. They're a temperature and texture decision. Keeping gelato covered and stored at the right depth protects its structure, slows ice crystal formation, and keeps every scoop as dense and creamy as the batch that came out of the machine that morning.

A tradition worth preserving

Our approach traces back to family recipes carried over from Northern Italy, where gelato-making has always been treated less like a dessert and more like a craft. Every batch is small, made fresh, and never held longer than it needs to be.

"The tub isn't just where we keep the gelato — it's part of how we make it taste right."

Modern display cases might look flashier, but they expose gelato to warmer air and constant light, which speeds up melting and dulls flavour. Pozzetti keep things cooler, denser, and closer to how gelato is meant to be enjoyed.

What it means for every scoop

When you order at Gelateria Cremona, you're getting gelato that's been resting the way it should — covered, cool, and cared for. It's a small detail, but it's one of the biggest reasons our gelato tastes the way it does: rich, smooth, and unmistakably Italian.
